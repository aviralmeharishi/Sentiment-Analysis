### 2. Constitutional Architecture: Interim vs. Full Union Budget

The presentation of an **Interim Budget** is governed by established parliamentary convention and constitutional provisions during the final year of an incumbent government's five-year mandate:

1. **Constitutional Basis (Article 116):**  
   Under Article 116 of the Constitution of India, the Lok Sabha exercises the authority to approve a **Vote on Account**. This mechanism constitutionally authorizes the withdrawal of funds from the Consolidated Fund of India solely to sustain basic administrative machinery, service sovereign obligations, and preserve fiscal continuity for a transitional window (typically 2 to 4 months) until the post-election government tables a comprehensive budget.

2. **Doctrine of Electoral Neutrality:**  
   Unlike a standard annual budget (Article 112), parliamentary ethics and convention proscribe the introduction of major fiscal overhauls, significant direct tax amendments, or sweeping statutory policy enactments that could compromise a level electoral playing field prior to general elections.
